## Design (pre-submission, 23 October 2026)

- Stakeholder: the fleet operations team of a robotaxi operator (such as Waymo) planning a possible New York City launch. Riders and the city regulator are secondary stakeholders.
- Decisions the project supports: which zones form the launch area; how many cars wait in each zone each hour; whether a car freed in a quiet zone stays or drives empty to a busier one.
- Main KPI: total profit. Others: profit per car per day, empty miles as a share of all miles, idle car-hours, share of requests served, gain over "copy last week", service gap between low- and high-demand zones.
- Hypotheses: H1 is tested on real trips; H2–H5 are tested in the fleet simulation on the test months.
- Data: NYC TLC High-Volume FHV trips for January–December 2024 (Uber/Lyft trips stand in for robotaxi demand), TLC zone lookup and shapefile (263 zones), NOAA hourly weather (Central Park; LaGuardia and JFK for checks), US OPM federal holidays. January 2025 is used only for the drift check.
- Not used: Waymo's public trip reports (location, time and mileage are redacted), Census income, NYC events data.
- Revenue = base passenger fare. Tips are excluded, because riders may not tip a robotaxi and tips are known only after the trip.
- Costs = recorded tolls + cost per mile × all miles + idle cost. $1.36 per mile (tested at $0.74 and $2.00); $2 per idle car-hour (tested at $0 and $5).
- Empty trips use real-trip median miles and minutes between zones; straight-line distance for zone pairs with few trips.
- Trip filters: duration 1 minute to 3 hours, speed 1–80 mph, request before pickup. Trips that share a key are flagged, not dropped. Row counts are checked after every join.
- Time split: train January–August 2024, validate September–October, test November–December. Holiday days are reported separately.
- Baselines: "copy last week", a ride-count plan, a fixed Manhattan plus Downtown Brooklyn area, and cars staying put.
- Plans are compared on the same test days with paired Wilcoxon tests on daily profit. A minimum service level applies to every zone.
- Each hypothesis is classed as supported, not supported or inconclusive.

## Zone neighbour table correction (2026-11-15) — Pending team review

- 2026-11-15 (Harsh Rohila, audit of Phase 1): the released `zone_neighbours` table (692 directed edges, 346 zone pairs) is incomplete. `build_zone_metadata` used shapely's exact `touches()` test, which is False when two zone shapes overlap by a numerical sliver (at most 0.1 m²) even though they share a long border. Recomputing from the unchanged TLC shapefile (SHA-256 `f6d71191…ef17`, the same file Himank recorded) found 651 neighbouring pairs; 305 real pairs were missing (for example Co-Op City and Eastchester, sharing about 5.6 km). The released graph had 43 connected groups of zones and 32 zones with no neighbour; the corrected graph has 10 groups and 6 zones with no neighbour (zones 1, 46, 103, 104, 105, 202). No test covered the neighbour logic, so the 57 Phase 1 tests did not catch it.
- Fix: new function `find_zone_neighbours` in `src/phase1_sources.py`. Two zones are neighbours when they are within 1 m (`NEIGHBOUR_TOLERANCE_METRES`); they share a border when at least 10 m (`MIN_SHARED_BOUNDARY_METRES`) of one outline lies within that tolerance of the other, otherwise it is a corner contact only. Five tests were added in `tests/test_phase1_sources.py` (shared border, tiny overlap, tiny gap, corner contact, distant zones).
- Result: 651 pairs = 1,302 directed edges (610 shared-border pairs, 41 corner-only pairs). All 346 old pairs are kept. The corrected table was rebuilt locally in `data/processed/phase1/zone_neighbours-fixed-20261115/zone_neighbours` with the same five columns; the released `phase1-2024-v1` table is untouched. Downstream work (launch-area choice, "move to a nearby zone") must use the corrected table, not the 692-edge one, until a corrected release is published. `shared_boundary_metres` is approximate (up to about 2 m too long per border); use it to rank borders only.
- Open issue for Phase 5: even the corrected graph has 10 groups, because zones across a river or bay share no polygon border (Manhattan, Bronx, Queens/Brooklyn, Staten Island, islands). A "connected launch area" crossing a river needs an explicit rule (for example bridge/tunnel links) agreed by the team.
